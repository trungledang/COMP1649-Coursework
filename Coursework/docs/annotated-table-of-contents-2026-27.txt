Below is an example of how a table of contents for your coursework report could look
like. You can use a different structure to what is shown here as long as the relevant
content is covered.
Your report should demonstrate sufficient English proficiency, critical and concise
writing, justifying choices, making clear arguments, structuring and professional
report formatting skills. In-text citations and references need to be correctly formatted
in Harvard style. You need to include at least 8 academic in-text citations/references
(for example, academic conference papers, journal papers, chapters from academic
books) to support your writing.
The word count for the report should be up to 3000 words (though 10% over is not
penalised). Title page, table of contents, references and appendices are not included
in the word count. The words should be counted by selecting the first word of the
Introduction chapter to the last word of the Conclusion chapter.
The format of the prototype must be an Axure file (.rp) or Figma Design (not Figma
Make) file, unless agreed with the lecturer otherwise.
1 Introduction
Brief introduction to the coursework and the structure of your report. Clearly state
your chosen topic from the options in the coursework specification.
2 Annotated bibliography
Write an annotated bibliography with reflective commentary on 6-8 relevant academic
HCI papers for the chosen coursework topic. The reflection needs to include a
personal reflection on why each source was chosen, how it connects to the chosen
coursework topic, and what implications can be drawn for the design of the prototype.
Begin each reflection with an in-text citation of the source and include the full
reference in the reference list (in References, see below).
Provide details of your search strategy and process of how you found your literature,
such as the databases and search terms used, in the appendix.
3 Product Design
3.1 Design
Describe the conceptual design of your proposed product idea. This high-level
description should go beyond the digital user interface alone. It may include your
assumptions, the main tasks the users will perform, and interaction types. The focus
should be on the product, the interface, and the interactions, and not the technical
attributes (e.g., hardware, operating systems etc.) of the product. You can include

2

sketches or visual representations to give the reader a rough idea of what the
product and overall solution will look like and discuss metaphors and icons. Show
clearly how your work has been informed by the outcomes from your literature
research on HCI Research and HCI Theory in the previous section.
Provide evidence of the design process and represent the development to concrete
design using one of the methods from chapter 12.5 Generating Prototypes in the
book Interaction Design: beyond human-computer interaction by Preece, Rogers,
and Sharp.
3.2 Design principles
Include a discussion of how design principles will be integrated in your (interface)
design with clear examples (with visual representations) of the interactions and
choices made. It is expected that the principles of visibility, feedback, constraints,
consistency, and affordance (as coined by Don Norman) are discussed as a
minimum. Provide clear examples, including visual examples of how you will
implement the principles.
4 Prototype
Briefly introduce and discuss your final mid-fidelity prototype and describe how the
design is informed by the research that has been carried out, as well as the concepts
and the principles discussed in the previous sections.
Include screenshots of the final mid-fidelity prototype (focusing on the digital user
interfaces) and provide explanations on your design process, such as any iterations
made, and the problems solved. Demonstrate how the design progressed over time
and discuss the purpose of your prototype.
Note: A series of static images is not a mid-fidelity prototype. Your submitted Axure
RP or Figma Design prototype needs to include interactivity that can be experienced
and tested by potential users, and that demonstrates the design interactions close to
how a real product would look.
There should be clear links between the coursework report and your prototype. There
needs to be evidence of the effective and successful application of Interaction Design
principles. Your design should be suitable as a tool to gain knowledge about the new
product for researchers and designers.
5 Research Study
This section should describe a proposal for a detailed (empirical) research study that
uses your prototype to test at least one assumption that you have made when
designing your prototype. You need to justify your decisions. In this step, you need to
present the setup of a research study, including the research question(s) or
hypothesis that your research study attempts to answer, and the material required to
run the study (e.g. questionnaires, interview questions, consent form templates etc.).
What hypothesis or research question are you investigating? Who are the participants
of your study and how will you recruit participants? How will the study be run, and
how will you analyse the data? You are not asked to run the study but only to
develop the proposed plan and the necessary research materials so that
someone else, such as a usability researcher could run the study using your

plan, materials, and your prototype. You need to create all necessary
documentation (e.g. a questionnaire, interview questions, consent form templates
etc.) that is required to run the study. The proposed plan needs to be included in the
body of the report and the material (e.g. questionnaires etc.) should be included in an
Appendix.
6 Conclusion
Provide a reflection on one (or more) key insight(s) and how it might influence your
future thinking or practice. E.g., reflect on how your understanding of HCI evolved,
what surprised you, or how the topic connects to your own experiences.
